About Us

In my opinion, Tokyo was and is the heart of world shipping. With this in mind I moved to Tokyo in October 1989 and established my first Japanese Shipping Line; Windward Passage Shipping KK. We operated one-way repositioning charters from Korea and Japan to South America with second-hand cars. On completion of our discharge, the ship loaded fishmeal and the like for return voyages to Japan and China. This was a mutually beneficial arrangement with our Australian partners. The business developed further to where Windward Passage Shipping KK become an important player in the breakbulk market from The Far East to Central and South American destinations where the business blossomed.

We were familiar with shipping vehicles and had a small but superb team of professionals and were encouraged to look at the Japan-NZ used car shipping trade. Then Kiwi Car Carriers KK was born in 1995 and was welcomed by the industry customers. Seeing the potential problems with biosecurity risk of seeds, plants and insects attaching to these cars, we made the fortuitous decision to establish a decontamination facility in Japan, in co-operation and agreement with the New Zealand Government. Inside of the four main Japanese ports, Kiwi Car Carriers KK had on-wharf facilities to decontaminate, store and survey prior to shipment, some 5,000 used vehicles so as to haver ‘cleaned cars’ arrive in New Zealand ports and thereby reducing the risk of spreading contaminants. The business flourished and after 6 years I sold out to HUAL, a Norwegian Car Carrier Shipping Line.

The family returned to New Zealand in 2000. After welcoming the time to rest from the previous 10 years, I decided to build a 44-metre passenger charter vessel, ‘Island Passage’ capable of 24 guests. Island Escape Cruises Ltd (not be confused with Island Escape Cruises NZ Ltd – who later purchased the company) operated within NZ during the spring and summer season, with focus on The Bay of Islands and Fiordland National Park. During the winter months we were in the South Pacific cruising around Vanuatu and Tahiti. The vessel was, in hindsight, 5 metres too short for the Pacific conditions and was unable to explore the many superb locations that were just beyond our reach. The ship was eventually sold to a Canadian interest and now operates comfortably in the sheltered Canadian fiords. The IEC business was sold. The new operators built a new ship at COVID-19 time, struggled to find enough able guests to comply with COVID-19 regulations before the company closed.

In 2020 I decided I was too young to retire but was going to keep my feet on dry land. Bissett Property Limited is developing residential property. Small subdivisions and full home renovations have been completed. Further developments are now in the planning.

I welcome the opportunity to debate and challenge old or new opportunities in the shipping, tourism and property arena. Please feel free to contact with me at any time.
